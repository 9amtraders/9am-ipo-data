Government policy and stocks: common questions

How do government policies affect stock prices in India?

A policy can raise a company's sales (a new scheme or government order), protect it from cheap imports (an anti-dumping duty, a quality control order or a certification rule) or raise its costs (a duty on its raw material). Prices often move when the market first hears of the policy, which can be well before the final notification.

What is an anti-dumping duty and which stocks benefit?

It is an extra import duty on goods sold into India below their fair price. DGTR investigates and recommends it, and the Finance Ministry decides whether to impose it. Indian makers of the same product usually benefit, while companies that use it as a raw material can lose.

Does a DGTR recommendation mean the duty will be imposed?

No. The Finance Ministry makes the final call, and in recent periods it has rejected most recommendations. That is why this page keeps recommendations in a separate "Waiting for a decision" list.

Should I buy a stock when a policy is announced?

Not just because of a headline. Check how big the benefit is compared with the company's revenue, whether the stock has already rallied on the news, and how long the policy will last. This page is for learning and research, not advice.
