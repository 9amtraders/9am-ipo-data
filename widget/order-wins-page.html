Order wins — explained simply

What is an order win announcement?

When a listed company receives a new order or contract that is material, SEBI rules require it to tell the stock exchange. On BSE these filings appear under "Company Update → Award of Order / Receipt of Order". This tracker collects all of them automatically through the day.

Why compare the order with market cap?

A ₹500 crore order is small news for a ₹50,000 crore company (1%) but huge for a ₹2,000 crore company (25%). Order value as a percentage of market capitalisation shows how much the order can really change the company's revenue, so the tracker flags orders worth 10% or more of market cap as "game-changers".

Do order wins make a stock go up?

Not always. Markets often expect orders in sectors like defence, railways and power, so the news may already be in the price. The "Do big orders move stocks?" section shows how stocks actually moved after orders of different sizes, from the close before the filing to the latest close.

What do Govt/PSU, Private and Overseas mean?

They describe who gave the order, guessed from the client's name and the filing text. Government and PSU orders (NHAI, Railways, NTPC, defence ministry) are usually safer to execute but slower to pay; overseas orders are converted to rupees at the day's exchange rate.

How often is this updated?

Every two hours on trading days from 10 AM to 8 PM IST, straight from BSE's announcement feed. It keeps about 3 months of history so you can see each company's full order pipeline.

Is the order value always correct?

No. Values are read from each filing's short summary, so some are missing, some include taxes and some are spread over several years. Treat the number as a guide and open the filing PDF to confirm.
